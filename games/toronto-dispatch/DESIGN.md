# Toronto Dispatch — design

## Player experience

You are a Toronto courier working a shift. Accept a job, collect the package, find a route, handle traffic, park, and deliver. The tension comes from route planning and controlled driving: braking early, choosing a usable lane, waiting for a streetcar, or taking a legal shortcut can outperform constant acceleration.

The city remains open between jobs. Landmarks help navigation; neighbourhoods change in building shapes, density, road width, trees, and industrial character. A compact readable city is the starting point for a larger world, not the full Toronto map on day one.

## Driving

Maintain ground-plane position, heading, signed speed, and collision footprint in a straight north-up top-down world. Acceleration changes speed over time. Releasing A allows rolling deceleration. B brakes; reverse becomes available only near rest. Turning has a speed-dependent limit. Trucks should require earlier braking; scooters should feel nimble but slower; motorcycles trade speed for vulnerability; cars provide the balanced baseline.

Prototype fixed-point values and a stable simulation tick after checking the engine. Diagonal motion must not be faster by accident. Screen and ground axes agree: east is right, north is up. Steering retains the existing left/right vehicle controls. Vehicle visuals and the collision footprint must agree, especially behind buildings.

Collision slows or stops the vehicle, applies a bounded time/cargo penalty, and offers recovery if stuck. Avoid rewarding repeated collisions or leaving the player trapped. Mission timeout must return the player to free roaming with a retry option.

The courier's car also wears: wall strikes by speed, car impacts by closing speed and kerb scrapes add damage (vans take half). From 40 the bonnet smokes, from 70 the engine fails with a warning, thicker smoke and three-quarter top speed, and at 100 the wreck crawls until supplies repair it; a stolen or changed vehicle is fresh. The HUD shows the car's condition while driving. Car condition is not saved.

## Street life

The city plays as a GTA-style sandbox around the courier work (accepted 2026-10-06; see [DECISIONS.md](DECISIONS.md)). Vehicles collide with momentum transfer and visible push, spin and rebound; struck pedestrians are thrown and fall without graphic detail. On foot the courier can punch, fire a pistol and steal road vehicles; a stolen car keeps its paint. The pistol aims with the D-pad, locks on to the nearest walker (or the pursuing patrol car) within about 45 degrees, marks it with red corner brackets and fires straight at it; holding B keeps firing. Rounds are long tracers with a white-hot head, drawn like an arcade run-and-gun shooter's, and check collisions every 4 px. Road vehicles are eight designs in seven paints and walkers six designs in four colours, picked whenever they rejoin the street out of view; cars are about one and a half people long. Police attention is shown as stars and grows with witnessed crimes and accumulated chaos: low levels bring arrests and fines, higher levels armed officers; running out of vitality ends at the hospital. An edge-of-screen pointer leads to the active objective. Tall downtown buildings overhang the street so walkers and cars pass behind them; footprints and collisions stay matched to the ground floor. A gunshot or a knocked-down walker sends the walkers nearby running. Spray bays are the way out of a pursuit, one in each scene: King St West (core), The Queensway (West), Bloor St West in Bloor West Village (High Park) and Queen St East between Carlaw and Pape (East). Stopping a car in one costs $25, clears the stars, repairs the car and repaints a stolen one; with stars on, a bay announces itself as the courier comes near.

Engine split: `td_life.c` (pedestrians, police, arrest/hospital), `td_drive.c` (vehicle handling, impacts, on-foot actions) and `td_life_draw.c` (presentation). Native SM83 kernels in `TORONTO.c` skip slots those modules own; street life runs once per rendered update so catch-up steps never cascade.

## Traffic and environment

Traffic follows authored lanes and respects signals. Intersections have a readable signal phase. The prototype has continuous road loops and a bus proxy; moving streetcars and full researched transit geometry remain planned. Streetcars should become larger obstacles on their own corridors, with a stopped boarding phase teaching the player to wait. Other planned obstacles include construction, parked vehicles, narrow passages, and crossings.

Route choice is the environmental advantage: connected lanes, avoiding queues, timing intersections and, since the parks revision (2026-10-07), cutting across flat ground. Cars and walkers can cross sidewalks, lawns, parks, plazas, lots and yards and pass under tree canopies; buildings, monuments, water, rails and walls block. Contract time limits still assume road routes. Add industrial-yard access and restricted missions after geography and collision are verified.

Cargo condition is part of the native contract rules. Damage starts after the first successful pickup; accepting work does not put the package or passenger in the vehicle. Traffic impacts, curb scrapes and head-on collisions then reduce condition; fragile art takes a larger curb/wall penalty. Passenger jobs use condition as comfort, with an additional penalty for steering at high speed while carrying the passenger. Completion pays the base reward scaled by remaining condition, plus a remaining-time bonus. Native tests verify the first-job payout and host checks cover passenger steering comfort; the other job types and reward balance still need full playtests. Weather traction, fuel and detailed traffic enforcement remain future options. Traffic rules need understandable feedback and tuning.

## Street life and police

The courier can punch, shoot a pistol and take a road vehicle; struck pedestrians are thrown and fall without gore. Crimes raise police attention in stars. Police are a pressure, not an instant end: the patrol car is slower than the courier's car at full speed and arrives from beyond the screen edge, officers on foot are slower than a walking courier below four stars, an arrest needs sustained contact, and shooting only starts at four stars with about one shot every 1.5 seconds. Attention cools a star every 12 seconds out of sight of police. One walker in eight is an officer, and the only patrol car is an ordinary blue car until a pursuit starts. An arrest costs $25 a star, half the ammunition and any active job; collapsing sends the courier to the core hospital for at most $60. Sidewalk pickups give cash, first aid or ammunition when walked or driven over; first aid and ammunition stay put when the courier is already full. Twenty lost parcels from the depot's past lie on lawns and plazas away from roads, five in each scene; each pays $50, the last a $500 bonus, and found parcels are saved. The exact parameters are recorded in [DECISIONS.md](DECISIONS.md); difficulty and pickup economy still need human playtests.

## Time of day and animation

The city runs a day/night cycle on the play clock: a game day lasts about 17 minutes and a new game starts at 08:00. Golden hour, a violet dusk, a navy night with sodium-lit sidewalks and a lilac dawn are palette sets derived from the daytime scene palettes, so collision art and occlusion never change; the HUD keeps its own colours and the pause menu shows the time with a sun or moon. From dusk to dawn the courier's vehicle lights the road ahead. Open water in every scene ripples (a shared 32 x 32 pixel texture, four frames, about four a second) with foam and sand at the shore, and a video screen plays on the roof at Yonge and Dundas. A vertical-blank interrupt copies the changed tiles, so the animation costs little frame time.

Animation marks what the player does: tyre smoke on hard braking, handbrake slides and sliding tails, exhaust on a launch, dust and sparks on impacts, a camera that looks ahead of a moving vehicle, punch and pistol poses, icons that rise from collected pickups, a parcel at each job pickup and a coin after a delivery, and a flashing light bar on the pursuing patrol car. Effects use two short-lived particle actors and the vehicle's own frames, and must not cost frame rate; see [DECISIONS.md](DECISIONS.md) and [TESTING.md](TESTING.md).

## Missions

Release target: at least two hours of varied gameplay. Build distinct job rules, route decisions and progression; do not pad the duration with mandatory idle waiting. Measure representative quest times and a campaign playthrough before claiming the target is met.

State flow: **available → accepted → pickup → carrying → delivered**. Failure and cancellation return to free roaming. Time starts at acceptance and pauses with the game. Pickup and delivery require the correct location, compatible vehicle, and a nearly stopped vehicle, plus an interaction press. Do not allow delivery through a wall or automatically complete at road speed.

Nothing covers the city by default. Up to two rows pop up from the bottom edge when there is something to say and sink after a few seconds: a notice, the B prompt at a station, the next stop when a job starts or advances, a new street name, and wanted stars while the police look; below that, ammunition after a shot, cash, vitality or car condition when they change, vitality while low, and the job's stop count, time and distance when the stop changes or under 15 seconds remain. Standing still shows the status line: after about a second on foot, about three seconds in a vehicle (so not at every red light). Waiting for or riding transit shows its three rows; the pause menu always shows cash, deliveries, the clock and the lost parcels found. Menus are bottom sheets over the paused city that show a few options at a time: the pause menu lists four of its nine actions with scroll marks and a hint line, and the dispatch board shows one contract under its chapter name. The paused city map shows the four registered areas together, with player, parked vehicle and objective markers. D-pad pans; A centres the job, booked transit stop or Union depot. Select cycles player/car/objective on foot and player/objective while driving. Browsing freezes the world and returns to the same camera and scene. The schematic is generated from registered collision ground at 1:8 scale, preserving road and foot routes; it represents existing playable coverage. Keep text short enough for the handheld screen. Rewards reflect completion, remaining time and cargo/passenger condition, so controlled driving matters to earnings. No payments or real courier integration.

The engine-integrated campaign is in `content/campaign.json`, compiled by `scripts/create_campaign.py`. The original three design samples in `content/missions.json` are historical planning material. Vehicle definitions express qualitative intended handling, not tuned simulation numbers.

## City coverage

The larger world is restricted to Old Toronto (the former City of Toronto), its waterfront and Toronto Islands. Use [the researched world reference](docs/TORONTO_RESEARCH.md) before drawing geography or landmarks. Island jobs must account for ferry access and restrictions on public vehicles.

The core (Dufferin to Broadview, Bloor to the harbour and the Islands) follows the City of Toronto Centreline: each kept street sits at its real position along the downtown grid, compressed so order and relative spacing hold, with arterial and local widths and minor streets omitted ([GEOGRAPHY.md](docs/GEOGRAPHY.md), `content/districts/core-research.json`). Blocks take the character of the neighbourhood at their real position, and landmarks sit where they are, from Union Station and the CN Tower to the ROM, Kensington Market, St Lawrence Market and the Distillery. Street links, landmarks and new district boundaries must keep using the official geography sources as coverage expands.

Parks are drawn from their real layout seen from above, enlarged where the compression would make them unreadable, with the features that make them recognisable at street level: Queen's Park as the ring of Queen's Park Crescent around the Legislative Building and the park with the King Edward VII statue (Centreline geometry, about three times wider than true scale); Trinity Bellwoods with its Queen St gates and dog bowl; Allan Gardens with the Palm House dome; Grange Park with The Grange; Sorauren Park's pitch and fieldhouse; High Park's long Grenadier Pond and zoo paddocks; Withrow Park's diamond and rink; Greenwood Park's pool. Sources and the source-derived facts are kept apart from the compressed design in the research files.

The current world has four local scenes: compressed central Toronto, Parkdale/Roncesvalles, High Park/Swansea/Junction and Riverside/Riverdale/Leslieville/western Danforth. Each is 1,024 × 976 pixels; the logical atlas is 4,096 × 976. The camera scrolls within the loaded scene, and native scene transitions connect 14 authored reciprocal seam pairs. Player and parked-car positions remain local to their districts, with shared mission, clock and progression state. Prototype 3's three-scene build verifies driving into the west, walking into High Park and back, recovering the parked car, returning to the core and restoring a western active job after reset. Candidate `1da71ba5…` loads all four actual native scenes and samples all three core/east approaches, core-to-west driving and west-to-High Park walking. A remote High Park reset restores nine completions, cash, world clock and the vehicle parked in the west; a local-map sample scrolls while player and clock remain frozen. Remaining seam lanes and handoffs still need native coverage.

The [western source/layout record](docs/WEST_DISTRICT.md) preserves College's termination at Dundas, the Roncesvalles/King bend, the Howard Park approach to Parkside, rail barriers and separate foot/vehicle waterfront access. The [eastern record](docs/EAST_DISTRICT.md) continues east of retained Broadview, without duplicating the Don: three core approaches connect Bloor/Danforth, Dundas and Queen. Gerrard remains conditional on correcting the core's College/Carlton east-bank label/topology. No King/Front or Lake Shore portal invents another bridge. Pape's researched pedestrian rail connection creates a walking alternative to vehicle detours. The artwork and bends are original compression informed by official City/TTC references. The larger [Old Toronto expansion plan](docs/OLD_TORONTO_EXPANSION.md) still proposes 17 districts covering the former municipality, waterfront and fuller Islands. Four compressed scenes do not complete that coverage, and the proposed 2026 era has not been adopted by the user.

Banked world metadata selects the next usable district seam through a directed graph filtered for driving or walking. It chooses the fewest feasible district hops, then compares approach distances; it is not a street-level shortest-path navigator. The beacon points to the next seam and the HUD names the next district instead of a distant client. Switching between walking and driving recomputes the marker and cached next-district cue for that mode. A missing vehicle route displays `NO ROAD ROUTE`; local foot-only deliveries display `PARK THEN WALK` when attempted in a vehicle. This adds no new transit/foot menu. Cross-district jobs and Withrow entry/exit supply sampled native cue evidence; complete HUD/input and handheld review remain pending.

Streetcars, subway stations, and railway corridors are part of the city identity. Scheduled subway, bus and ferry travel is part of the published prototype; current source also implements a representative 501 Queen timetable across west, core and east. Transit route identity must be researched separately from current service detours.

## Scheduled transit

The courier can park, pay an in-game fare at a real named stop, and ride autonomous trains or TTC buses to a strategic drop-off point. Service timing advances on the game clock; the transit choice screen displays the next departure using the same two-second boarding window as the waiting state. Confirming during an open window boards immediately rather than waiting for another simulation second. Otherwise the courier waits until the shared schedule opens. Timers continue while waiting or riding; menus/pause freeze simulation. Vehicles remain parked and must be recovered. Heavy cargo and passenger jobs may require the assigned road vehicle; small packages can be carried on foot and transit. Route names follow official references, but frequency, travel time and fares are fictional gameplay values. Final candidate `1da71ba5…` verifies an open-window subway selection, immediate boarding, one fare deduction from 30 to 27, and arrival on foot at King while the car remains parked at Union. Reset during a paid ride remains separate from this arrival sample; exact build evidence belongs in [TESTING.md](TESTING.md). Existing scheduled Line 1, 94 bus and ferry service points retain their routes and fares. Current source adds service 4, the [501 Queen game timetable](docs/STREETCAR.md), at eight supplemental sidewalk platforms numbered 43–50 across west, core and east. Each shared platform consolidates directional stop references; all 43 original records remain unchanged. The menu derives eastbound or westbound from the selected destination and displays the departure countdown, ride duration and fare. A trip costs three game dollars; each direction has a 32-second period (64 before 2026-10-07) with a two-second window, and ride time is four seconds per stop interval, at most 28 seconds. Timetable phase comes from the world clock without running remote actors. Original curb signs identify the platforms and stations. On foot near a station the HUD offers B for its service; menus never offer the courier's own stop, a short fare is refused on confirmation, and buses, streetcars and ferries visibly arrive at their berths (ferries over open water). Current diversions, a complete 501 route, King 504 and Line 2 boarding are outside this implementation; the map era remains unadopted.

The Queen source keeps the existing 58-byte version-6 save layout. Saved origin/target validation uses the same banked transit module as the UI and runtime. Paid arrivals commit the alighted state before a destination scene is queued; allocation failure retains the paid ride for retry. An earlier Queen candidate completed three cross-district rides and paid reset/map/car-recovery checks, but a separate legacy Union journey placed the courier atop their own parked car. The source correction chooses a nearby, connected walkable alighting point clear of the parked car and loaded traffic, or retains the paid trip for retry if all candidates are blocked. The corrected source passes host fixtures; the exact final native candidate verifies three Queen journeys, paid map/reset, legacy subway/bus/ferry travel and clear Union alighting with walking/car recovery. This is the Prototype 6 milestone; unplayed platforms/conditions need further coverage. [TESTING.md](TESTING.md) preserves the exact predecessor and source-check scope.

## Story and dispatch radio

The campaign tells an original story: Margo's independent depot by Union Station against Rushly, an app courier, with the city's Lakelight lantern festival contract as the prize. Rosa runs dispatch; Margo, 22 clients and city contacts across the neighbourhoods, Dev (a Rushly rider who changes sides) and Vance (Rushly's city manager) call in. Calls appear as a card above the pop-up rows, with Rosa's portrait or a caller silhouette, the speaker's name card and three typed rows, while play continues; menus suspend a call.

Each contract has a briefing on accepting it, a line at the first pickup and the client's answer on delivery. Each chapter opens with a call once its contracts unlock, and plot turns play right after the contract they follow. Chapters open in story order: the next chapter waits for the previous chapter's spine contract as well as the delivery count, and the board shows "AFTER JOB n" until then. The radio also covers police attention (with a hint about the spray bay), arrests, the hospital, nightfall, morning and chatter on quiet stretches of free roam. Chatter follows the campaign: mid-campaign and festival-week lines join the general ones once their chapters open. Clients sometimes react to a delivery that arrives dented or well ahead of time, and the lost parcels carry a short thread about Ernie, Margo's first courier. Dialogue lives in `content/story.json` (premise, speakers, chapters, contract calls) and `content/radio.json` (beats and other calls); `scripts/create_radio.py` generates the banked text and `scripts/check_story.py` checks places, route order and story order.

## Art and sound

Use original low-resolution pixel art: readable vehicles, warm brick blocks, glass towers, storefronts, streetcar rails, tree canopies, and distinct landmark silhouettes. Keep camera orientation consistent and make the player's vehicle visible behind tall buildings using techniques supported by the engine. Road markings, signals, mission markers, and collision boundaries must remain readable at native resolution.

The target Game Boy Color display is 160 × 144 pixels. Palette, tile, sprite, VRAM, scene, and ROM limits must be checked against the installed toolchain; do not design a desktop-resolution asset pack and scale it down later. Reserve screen space for a small HUD without hiding approaching traffic.

The native prototype now includes the original City Shift score, vehicle engine pitches, braking noise and impact, pickup, completion, transit, failure and menu cues. The pause menu offers music + effects, effects only and silent modes. Captured native PCM verifies output and mode behaviour; human listening, mix quality, and physical speaker/headphone checks remain pending. See [audio source and integration](docs/AUDIO.md). Moving-streetcar audio remains part of the planned streetcar milestone.

## Engine feasibility before production

Use the ModRetro plugin's GB Studio workflow. Native tests must establish top-down backgrounds, vehicle control, collision, walking, roof/canopy occlusion, and car entry. Standard exploration scenes may need an engine extension for this combination. Inspect supported extension hooks and build/resource limits before committing to a whole city. If the chosen workflow cannot support the accepted design, present the tested constraint and viable options to the user.

Do not claim the concept or content scaffold is playable. ROM compilation, emulator gameplay, live streaming, and physical cartridge boot each need separate evidence.

## Current implementation and tuning

The four compiled north-up scenes contain 211 building footprints: 80 preserved core buildings, 37 western, 49 High Park/Junction and 45 eastern. Roads use 48-pixel asphalt and 8-pixel sidewalks on each side. Six architectural styles and original carhouse, Regency cottage, pavilion, Junction brick, theatre, heritage-house and factory interpretations add district character. Generic building shadows/priority are clipped at asphalt; intentional core canopies and CN raised lips retain depth occlusion over passable ground. Added-district priority is clear of asphalt. Native collision checks every tile overlapped by the car footprint, blocking buildings, water and rail barriers. CGB tile priority hides sprites beneath raised roof lips and tree canopies. Walking shows directional steps, parking places the courier beside the door, and A animates re-entry near the parked vehicle. Eastern source art fits 85 raw / 63 flip-canonical tiles under its 320-tile target; all four actual scenes load in the current native sample. Exhaustive occlusion and crowded-scene review remain separate gates.

Velocity eases toward steering direction, yaw advances on a dedicated speed-dependent counter, and glancing curb contact slides along the free axis. When a quantised corner catches the footprint, held acceleration with no brake and forward speed at least 3 can apply a 1–6 pixel lateral correction. Bounds, the candidate footprint and the swept lateral path must all be clear; assistance occurs at most once per rendered update. Broad head-on walls still stop the vehicle. Braking takes priority when both buttons are held.

Traffic motion runs independently of its screen presentation. The working world has 620 fixed pedestrian routes, with eight nearby walkers in the loaded district, each drawn with one 8x16 hardware sprite. A banked selector retains eight identities in a 32-byte coordinate cache and re-picks at most two per refresh. Six road vehicles per district follow their loops; a vehicle that drifts far out of view rejoins its own loop just beyond the screen edge, heading toward the courier, so nearby streets stay busy without more actors. Banked world names, portals and traffic data use an additional 36-byte six-vehicle sample cache and one next-district byte, rather than keeping all world tables in the fixed ROM bank. The three added scenes have six closed traffic loops each, 18 total. Traffic and people continue during transit waiting/riding; pause/map/menu screens freeze the world. Remote districts are unloaded; route counts do not imply all actors run simultaneously. Eight VM contexts and the 58-byte version-6 save state remain unchanged by this expansion.

Simulation compensates for skipped render frames with bounded substeps; the clock follows video frames. Native driving regressions cover the first job, the previously stopping Distillery turn at speed 24, parking and re-entry. Prototype 3 adds sampled reciprocal district travel, remote parked-car recovery and remote active-job reset. The final four-scene candidate repeats the held-turn sequence at speed 24, completes nine distinct jobs, verifies the Withrow parking/walking cue and restores remote High Park progression after reset. Full `make check` passes 2,352 engine, 2,974 bridge, 16,997 navigation and nine memory fixtures; the actual ROM guard leaves 1,484 bytes below stack base `DF00`. Host logic, linked allocation and native samples remain separate evidence.
