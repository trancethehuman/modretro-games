# Toronto Dispatch — design

## Player experience

You are a Toronto courier working a shift. Accept a job, collect the package, find a route, handle traffic, park, and deliver. The tension comes from route planning and controlled driving: braking early, choosing a usable lane, waiting for a streetcar, or taking a legal shortcut can outperform constant acceleration.

The city remains open between jobs. Landmarks help navigation; neighbourhoods change in building shapes, density, road width, trees, and industrial character. A compact readable city is the starting point for a larger world, not the full Toronto map on day one. The accepted direction includes a GTA-inspired street simulation around the courier: visible civilian pedestrians, collision consequences, service vehicles, drivers obeying lights and activity on the waterfront. The user selected chaotic sandbox play with escalating police pursuits and tougher penalties. Current source integrates bounded road pursuits and the first street systems below; scoped native street functionality passes, while broader pacing/gameplay and hardware remain pending. Geographic scope stays within Old Toronto.

## Driving

Maintain ground-plane position, heading, signed speed, and collision footprint in a straight north-up top-down world. Acceleration changes speed over time. Releasing A allows rolling deceleration. B brakes; reverse becomes available only near rest. Turning has a speed-dependent limit. Trucks should require earlier braking; scooters should feel nimble but slower; motorcycles trade speed for vulnerability; cars provide the balanced baseline.

Prototype fixed-point values and a stable simulation tick after checking the engine. Diagonal motion must not be faster by accident. Screen and ground axes agree: east is right, north is up. Steering retains the existing left/right vehicle controls. Vehicle visuals and the collision footprint must agree, especially behind buildings.

Collision slows or stops the vehicle, applies a bounded time/cargo penalty, and offers recovery if stuck. Avoid rewarding repeated collisions or leaving the player trapped. Mission timeout must return the player to free roaming with a retry option.

## Expanded street simulation — scoped native prototype, broader acceptance pending

The user requests recognisable ordinary people on foot, player vehicle impacts with gameplay consequences, police/fire/ambulance vehicles, trucks, buses, water boats, bridges, road-driving NPCs and traffic lights those drivers follow. Chaotic sandbox play, escalating police pursuits and tougher penalties are accepted. The first implementation uses a non-graphic stumble; that existing recovery does not adopt lethal injury, combat, weapons or stealing NPC vehicles.

Two original civilian variants have separate walk cycles and a stumble pose. Swept player-vehicle movement checks visible humans; a newly struck person holds their route position for six active world seconds and resumes the same walking phase afterward. That person cannot generate another charge during the stumble. A contact update halves current velocity and adds one attention level per newly struck human, capped at three. The fine is $20 × hit count × resulting attention: isolated impacts at levels one/two/three cost $20/$40/$60. Cash cannot become negative. Each person removes ten carried-condition points only after pickup; driving conduct still costs money before pickup. Pause/maps/menus freeze recovery. The per-person recovery is transient and resets on district load; its money, cargo and attention consequences are saved.

The HUD's `H` shows attention from zero to three. Human impacts refresh a 30-second quiet countdown. A police vehicle within 96 pixels on both axes refreshes that countdown while the courier roams; away from it, each 30 active seconds removes one level. Capture occurs within 32 pixels on both axes, covering the junction's 24-pixel stop line plus the bounded eight-pixel advance: velocity becomes zero, the fine is $25 × H² ($25/$100/$225), cash is clamped at zero and attention clears. Pause/maps/menus freeze the clock. Player red-light fines remain separate and do not yet increase attention. This supersedes the earlier $20-per-person/$15×H/24-pixel patrol rules; exact balance and native capture/escape acceptance remain pending. The rules are fictional gameplay, not Toronto enforcement policy.

One loaded police vehicle uses cardinal waypoints on actual collision-backed roads, selecting connected junction exits toward the courier while preserving road, water and rail barriers. Desired pursuit advance is `(8 + 4H)` Q4 units per active VBlank, capped at eight pixels per sweep. The six-byte cached plan, four-byte origin and one-byte stuck counter add eleven transient bytes; it replans on waypoint arrival or after 64 blocked updates. Ordinary signal, occupied-junction, tram, pedestrian and vehicle checks remain active. When pursuit ends, it drives back to its authored patrol through connected roads rather than teleporting. State flow: **available → accepted → pickup → carrying → delivered**. Failure and cancellation return to free roaming. Time starts at acceptance and pauses with the game. Pickup and delivery require the correct location, compatible vehicle, and a nearly stopped vehicle, plus an interaction press.
